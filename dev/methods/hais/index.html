Hamiltonian importance sampling

Native HMC moves proposal centres using target gradients and a Metropolis correction. The returned observations remain importance samples drawn from normalized lower proposals. HMC trajectory points are not additional output samples.

Centre exploration alone does not fit lower widths. When moments exist, mixture covariance includes both component covariance and the spread of component centres. Wide lower proposals can therefore become overdispersed as centres explore the target, especially in many dimensions. Cooperation can also remove centres from a minor mode. Check weight concentration and mode coverage rather than acceptance alone.

Example

This three-dimensional target has quartic tails. Its coordinate means are zero.

using ImportanceSamplers, Random, Statisticslogtarget(z) = -sum(x -> x^4, z) / 4 - sum(abs2, z) / 2score!(g, z) = (g .= .-z.^3 .- z; nothing)target = LogTarget(logtarget; grad=score!)bank = ProposalBank([    SphericalStudentT(7.0, [-1.0, 0.0, 0.0], 1.0),    SphericalStudentT(7.0, [ 1.0, 0.0, 0.0], 1.0),])upper = HamiltonianMonteCarlo(;    step_size=0.15, leapfrog_steps=8, tuning=WarmupTuning(32),)algorithm = HAIS(bank; transition=upper, rounds=5, round_size=2_000)prepared = prepare_sampler(Xoshiro(42), target, algorithm)samples = importance_sample!(prepared)(mean=mean(samples), draws=length(samples))
(mean = [0.004102943614662733, 0.0007766654163162547, 0.004190516557087423], draws = 10000)

Explicit gradients, a selected DifferentiationInterface backend, and first-order LogDensityProblems targets use the existing gradient interface. Lower sample weighting evaluates the log target without requesting a gradient.

With ForwardDiff installed, select it explicitly instead of supplying grad=:

using ADTypes, ForwardDiffad_target = LogTarget(logtarget, AutoForwardDiff())ad_samples = importance_sample(Xoshiro(44), ad_target, algorithm)

For native CUDA, transfer a fresh complete prepared sampler before its first run:

using CUDAimport MLDataDevicesCUDA.allowscalar(false)device = MLDataDevices.with_eltype(MLDataDevices.CUDADevice(), nothing)gpu_sampler = device(prepare_sampler(Xoshiro(45), target, algorithm))gpu_samples = importance_sample!(gpu_sampler)host_samples = MLDataDevices.CPUDevice()(gpu_samples)

The target and explicit score must support device execution. Ancillary arrays passed through the target context move with the prepared sampler. Metal and Reactant HMC execution are not enabled.

HMC controls

step_size and leapfrog_steps are required. Trajectory length stays fixed. Optional warmup tunes one shared step size from acceptance probabilities, then freezes it. Warmup moves add cost and return no importance samples. Successful reuse does not repeat them.

mass is the momentum covariance, not the lower proposal covariance. It accepts an isotropic scalar, I, Diagonal, an SPD matrix, or its Cholesky factorization. Dense operations use factors and solves, not matrix inversion. Mass does not adapt. Lower scales adapt only with adapt_scales=true.

Every positive vector dimension is supported. A one-dimensional problem uses [x], not scalar-layout proposals. Named constraint transforms retain their existing score pullback and Jacobian conventions.

Both require equal positive masses and a round size divisible by the proposal count. Gaussian, Student-t, and independent Laplace lower scales stay fixed by default. All lower rounds enter the result with their original current-mixture weights.

Width adaptation

Enable marginal-width fitting independently of the upper transition:

adaptive = HAIS(bank; transition=upper, rounds=5, round_size=2_000,                adapt_scales=true)fitted = prepare_sampler(Xoshiro(46), target, adaptive)adaptive_samples = importance_sample!(fitted)snapshot = current_proposal(fitted)mean(adaptive_samples)
3-element Vector{Float64}:
  0.022168770246893614
  0.010947678465243547
 -0.009121776849242941

The same keyword works with any LAIS transition. This is a package extension, not a covariance update specified by the HAIS paper. It changes neither HMC mass nor the centre update.

For each generating group, normalize its current-round DM weights. Compute its weighted coordinate means and centred variances, without a Bessel correction. Each new marginal variance is half the previous variance plus half the fitted variance. Gaussian scale squared equals variance. Student-t scale squared is variance times (nu - 2) / nu, requiring nu > 2. Laplace scale squared is half the variance. Every Student-t component retains its degrees of freedom.

These local moments estimate a density proportional to π(x)qⱼ(x)/ψ(x), not the entire target independently for each proposal. Diagonal proposals fit each marginal width. Factor proposals scale their factor rows, preserving correlations. New correlations are not fitted. Spherical inputs can become diagonal proposals in current_proposal.

All-zero groups, computed local weight ESS below two, zero or nonfinite fitted variances, and unrepresentable updated parameters retain that component's previous widths. An invalid coordinate rejects the complete component update. No estimator weights are clipped, tempered, or recomputed.

Widths change only after a lower round. HAIS first completes cooperation against the unchanged generating bank. Each destination then uses its own generating group's width fit, regardless of the selected ancestor. The final fitted widths remain available for reuse, snapshots, and retarget. Failed calls preserve the pre-call widths. Native CUDA fits remain on-device without additional numerical host reads. Reactant width adaptation is not enabled.

Width adaptation can reduce overdispersion, but cannot guarantee adequate local ESS or preserve a minor mode lost through centre cooperation. Inspect weight and coverage diagnostics across independent runs. Narrow Gaussian proposals can leave heavy target tails poorly covered despite plausible fitted variances.

Cooperation and weights

For the lower bank in round t, define

\[\psi_t(x)=\frac1M\sum_{j=1}^M q_{t,j}(x), \qquad \log w=\log\pi(x)-\log\psi_t(x).\]

After lower weighting, HAIS moves each centre with HMC and selects centres independently with replacement. Selection scores are proportional to π(y)/ψₜ(y), evaluated at each endpoint against the unchanged old bank. Destination slots retain their scales and IDs.

These are adaptation scores, not importance weights for the HMC endpoint law. The selected centres need not follow the target. Estimation uses only lower draws with their actual proposal mixture. Under support and integrability assumptions, the fixed-budget unnormalized integral estimator is unbiased. Self-normalized means and log-normalizer estimates are generally biased at finite sample size.

The implementation interprets the paper's inconsistent denominator indices pointwise. The 1/M mixture normalization matters for evidence, even though it cancels in normalized means.

For example, exact standard-normal HMC endpoints reweighted by a standard-normal target divided by a Normal(0, sqrt(2)) lower density converge to variance 2/3, not one. This illustrates why the centre-law claim differs from the valid lower estimator.

Diagnostics and failures

samples.diagnostics.transition separates initial, warmup, and production target/gradient work. It also records acceptance, support rejections, energy divergences, and the retained step size. Include upper work and warmup when comparing complete-method performance.

A finite energy error above divergence_threshold records a divergence without changing MH acceptance. An invalid numerical trajectory rejects. A malformed target or gradient fails the call. Failure to represent a logical value or Jacobian under a configured transform also fails the call, even when sampled coordinates remain finite. Failures preserve pre-call proposal and tuning state, but do not rewind the RNG. All-zero lower batches remain in the result. Their normalized summaries are undefined.

See Reuse for proposal snapshots and target replacement.
